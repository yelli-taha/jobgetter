# Cover Letter Rules

Claude follows every rule here. When I correct a letter and say "save this to the rules", the correction goes
here so every future letter follows it. Setup fills in my preferences; the defaults below are a good start.

## Hard limits
- One page, never more. Cut words; never shrink the font or margins.
- Salutation and sign-off: as in me/profile.json (default "Dear Hiring Manager," ... "Sincerely," + my name).
- No paragraph headers. No em dashes. No bullet points.
- Never invent experience, numbers, or details that aren't in my files.
- About 250-450 words in the body (set in profile.json).

## Structure (a default, not a formula)
1. Opening: start with something specific about me and this role, not a generic "I am applying for..." line.
2. Experience: the one to three most relevant things I've done, with results. Choose per job; don't list everything.
3. Why this company: written fresh every time (below).
4. Closing: short and confident, tied to the role.

## Voice
- Every sentence should sound like me: confident but grounded, plain words, no buzzwords.
- No industry jargon or company vocabulary I wouldn't naturally use. Borrowed jargon reads as researched, not genuine.
- Never write ideas or opinions I haven't expressed myself. If unsure, leave it out or flag it for me.
- Lead with strengths. If I don't meet every requirement, say what I bring and how I'd close the gap.

## The "Why this company" paragraph
- Built from my own values, stories and experience (me/stories/stories.md), not an outsider's summary of the company.
- Balance three things: why I'm drawn to this kind of work, what the company actually does, and what this role needs.
- No flattery. Flag it "Your review: why-company" so I can check it sounds like me.

## Leave out
- Anything in the Private section of stories.md.

## Banned phrases
(Also in me/checks.json, so the builder catches them.)
- "I am writing to apply", "I am excited to apply", "fast-paced environment", "hit the ground running",
  "team player", "think outside the box", "I believe I would be a great fit", "testament to".
